# Moving prescriptive process monitoring onto the sensor clock

The thesis question has not been answered yet, though most of its parts exist in neighbouring literatures. Six independent searches (PresPM, IoT process mining, decision granularity in RL, causal ML for continuous and time-varying treatments, benchmarks and offline RL, manufacturing prescriptive analytics) found **no prescriptive process monitoring (PresPM) work that places decision points anywhere other than at events, prefixes or prespecified process points, and no prescriptive method of any kind on IoT-enriched event logs**. The closest PresPM works, Bozorgi et al. 2023 ("after each event"), Weytjens et al. 2023 (one timed intervention over event states) and SCOPE (arXiv 2512.17629, decision points that are "prespecified or discovered"), all keep time on the event clock. The closest work on granularity comes from RL and biostatistics. Metelli et al. 2020 choose an action-persistence level from batch data. Zhang et al. 2023 prove that an optimal time step exists for a given data budget. Frost et al. 2026 show that temporal binning cuts deployed offline-RL performance by up to 60% and inflates OPE estimates 1.5–3x. Sun & Crawford show that discretization bias in causal estimands does not go away with more data. None of these uses event logs, none compares an exogenous, non-uniform event grid against a uniform sensor grid, and none builds an OPE-based selector of decision granularity. The defensible novelty claim is therefore the combination: offline, causal or off-policy learning of start/stop timing from IoT-enriched event logs, with decision granularity (event-aligned, fixed-interval, sensor-tick, learned) as the experimental variable, plus a data-driven selector and a semi-synthetic benchmark with ground-truth timing effects. Acting at every sensor sample is not new in itself, since process-control RL does it routinely. The novelty is in the data regime (confounded logs), the comparison, and the selector. The novelty verdict rests on web, arXiv and publisher searches, not on full DBLP or proceedings sweeps, so the 2025–2026 BPM/ICPM workshop proceedings and the Bertrand/Serral IoT group's newest work still need a direct check.

**Verification legend (preserved from the research notes).** **[V]** means the primary page (arXiv, DOI, publisher, PMLR, Zenodo) was fetched, or a search returned it with matching metadata. **[V-meta]** means title, authors, venue and link were verified, but the content came from snippets or mirrors. **[V-partial]** means existence or the ID was seen, but title or authors are not fully confirmed. **[S]** means secondhand: cited inside another paper or taken from the researcher's prior knowledge, with a link that was not opened. Where two notes gave the same paper different tags, this report keeps the stronger tag.

## Seven near-misses, and how the thesis differs from each

The novelty question is best answered paper by paper. The table lists the works a thesis examiner is most likely to raise, what each actually does, and the specific axis on which the thesis departs from it. The pattern across the table is consistent. PresPM works have the right data (event logs) and the right goal (interventions) but the wrong clock. RL and biostatistics works have the right concern (granularity) but the wrong data regime: online simulators, LQR theory, clinical binning, or effect estimation of fixed regimes rather than policy learning.

Several more works sit one step further out and should still be cited and contrasted. **Burns, Yu, Finn & Hausman** show that mixing offline data at different control frequencies destabilises Q-learning, and that scaling the N in N-step returns to the discretization beats naive mixing by about 50% ([arXiv 2207.13082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13082), [V]). They handle mixed frequencies but do not select one. **Treven et al. (TaCoS, NeurIPS 2024)** recast costly-interaction continuous-time RL as a discrete MDP where the policy picks an action and a hold duration, cutting interactions at equal or better return, but online ([arXiv 2406.01163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01163), [V]). **Wald et al. 2025 (EDQ)** and **Chen et al. 2024** avoid a grid entirely by treating decision times as a point or renewal process for off-policy evaluation ([2503.15890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15890), [V]; [2409.09236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09236), [V]). They are the "no-discretization" alternative to a selector and a strong evaluation option. **Schulam & Saria 2018** show that discretizing logged interaction data biases decision-making models ([arXiv 1810.03025](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03025), [V]). **Khorasgani et al. 2021 (Hitachi)** learn continue-versus-maintain policies offline from logs combining sensor time series and discrete events ([arXiv 2109.15050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.15050), [V]). That is the closest data-regime match outside process mining, though it does not vary granularity.

## PresPM learned when to intervene, but only on the event clock

PresPM's own literature already treats timing as a first-class problem, which makes the thesis an extension of an existing debate rather than a new one. The standard survey, Kubrak, Milani, Nolte & Dumas's "Quo vadis?" (PeerJ CS 2022, 37 papers), classifies methods by objective, intervention type, modelling technique, data input and policy. It calls for causal, real-world-validated methods. Its data-input dimension is event-attribute based, with no sensor category ([PeerJ](https://peerj.com/articles/cs-1097), [V]). No 2024–2026 PresPM survey synthesising the causal and RL work turned up.

Four timing strands run through the field, and every one measures time as prefix length or event index. The first is alarm-based monitoring: Teinemaa et al. 2018 optimise an alarm threshold over outcome predictions per prefix ([arXiv 1803.08706](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08706), [S]). Fahrenkrog-Petersen et al.'s "Fire Now, Fire Later" (KAIS 2022) adds a cost model with intervention effectiveness that decays, and shows that thresholds that change with case progress lower net cost ([arXiv 1905.09568](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09568), [V-meta]). The second is Metzger's reliability-triggered proactive adaptation, learned by online RL. It reports about 8% lower cost than empirical thresholding ([UDE summary](https://sse.uni-due.de/aktuelles/alle-news-insights/artikel/anpassung-von-geschaeftsprozessen-durch-online-reinforcement-learning), [V-meta]), and an Information Systems 2023 comparison treats the earliness-accuracy trade-off explicitly on four logs ([arXiv 2307.05939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05939), [V-meta]). The third is causal "when to treat": Bozorgi et al.'s CATE-at-prefix work with orthogonal random forests ([arXiv 2105.07111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07111), [V-meta]), its CAiSE 2023 RL extension, Shoush & Dumas's resource-constrained causal triggering ([2109.02894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02894), [V-meta]), conformal triggering ([2212.03710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03710), [V]) and RL triggering ([2307.06564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06564), [V-meta]). The fourth is Weytjens et al.'s timed single intervention. In this family, treatments are binary or small categorical sets. Even SimBank's interest rate has only three levels ([SCOPE HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17629)). **No continuous-dose treatment appears anywhere in PresPM**, so the thesis's original dose-response framing is itself an open gap.

RL-based PresPM splits into online RL on simulators (Metzger, Weytjens, Shoush) and MDPs or offline RL learned from logs. Branchi et al. build an MDP from past executions ([2203.15398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15398), [V-meta]) and later cluster prefix states with KMeans plus Q-learning, the "KMeans-Q" baseline SCOPE uses ([DBLP search](https://dblp.org/search?q=Recommending+the+optimal+policy+by+learning+to+act+from+temporal+data), [S]). A CQL-with-LSTM comparison has appeared in Information Systems 2026 ([arXiv 2303.09209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09209), [V-meta]). FORLAPS does offline RL with process-aware augmentation and assumes no unobserved confounding ([2501.10543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10543), [V-meta]). Weinzierl et al. frame the problem as next best actions ([2008.08693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08693), [V-meta]). **In every formulation the MDP step is an event or activity.** No PresPM paper uses a semi-MDP, options, or continuous-time RL.

Evaluation is the field's admitted weak point, and the KU Leuven group's benchmark line is the infrastructure the thesis inherits. SimBank provides full ground truth, ProCause generates counterfactuals fitted to real logs, and SCOPE's SimBPIC17 is a reusable semi-synthetic sequential benchmark. Weytjens et al. argue that most PresPM experiments on historical data compromise evaluation and rule out online RL ([2306.04299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04299)). **None of these benchmarks contains sensor streams or allows interventions between events.** No formal OPE estimators (IS, DR, FQE) were found in PresPM papers, which instead rely on simulators or generated counterfactuals. Bringing horizon-robust OPE into PresPM would therefore be a secondary contribution in its own right.

## IoT process mining stops at abstraction and prediction

The IoT-BPM community is small and tightly connected: TUM and Vienna (Mangler, Rinderle-Ma, Ehrendorfer), Trier/DFKI (Grüger, Malburg, Bergmann), KU Leuven (Bertrand, Serral), St. Gallen (Seiger, Weber), Rome (Leotta, Mecella) and Koschmider. It names granularity mismatch as its core problem, but frames it as **turning sensor data into events, never as deciding between events**. The 2020 manifesto (Janiesch et al., IEEE SMC Magazine; [arXiv 1709.03628](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.03628), [S]) set the agenda. Mangler et al. 2024 state that sensor data is "typically at a lower granularity" than process events. They pose three questions (which sensor values form an event, which mark its start and end, which are related but non-essential) and define a spectrum of process awareness ([arXiv 2405.08528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08528), [V]). Brzychczy et al.'s 2025 KAIS review of 36 papers names abstraction into discrete events as the core challenge ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10115-024-02297-y), [V]). Across these agendas the listed challenges are granularity mismatch, continuous versus discrete data, abstraction and correlation, data quality, inadequate log formats, and dataset scarcity. "Decision points between events" is not among them.

Formats evolved from SensorStream XES (2022; [2206.11392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11392), [V]) to DataStream XES (Future Internet 2023), NICE (ICPM 2023 workshops) and CORE, an object-centric core metamodel on OCEL 2.0 ideas ([arXiv 2506.21300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21300), [V]). All of them attach streams to events or objects, so decision-relevant information exists between events but no decision point is defined there. CORE notes that sensor data is noisy, voluminous and collected independently of the process, which makes integration a major obstacle ([2506.21300 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21300v3)). Event abstraction is the most active subtopic. Methods include clustering raw sensor values (Ehrendorfer et al., CoopIS 2023), the Radiant DSL for online detection ([2507.00686](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00686v2), [S]), HMM-based Sensor2EventLog ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-28110-4_10), [S]), and LLM labelling in IoT Miner ([2509.05769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05769), [V]). The thesis can argue that abstraction **discards exactly the inter-event information that timing decisions need**. These pipelines can also serve as the generators of the event-aligned decision grid in its comparisons.

IoT-enhanced *predictive* monitoring exists but is thin, and it still predicts at event timestamps. The reference most relevant to the thesis is Bertrand, Stevens, Deforce, De Smedt, De Weerdt & Serral, "Approaches for IoT-enhanced predictive process monitoring" (Process Science 2025; [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44311-025-00011-x), [V-meta]). According to search snippets, it proposes adding IoT-derived features to events, converting IoT data into context events, or analysing both jointly, with 30 IoT features built from 5 sensor series. The abstract could not be read past the paywall. Related work includes Ehrendorfer et al.'s runtime outcome assessment with context data (ICSOC 2021, [S]) and Stertz et al.'s sensor-based concept drift (BPM 2020, [S]).

The datasets are ready even though the methods are not. The IoPT Zenodo community ([zenodo.org/communities/iopt](https://zenodo.org/communities/iopt), [V]) hosts the TUM **cotton candy log** (2025, XES SensorStream format). It records a UR5e robot, the machine, environment and internal temperatures, and a precision scale. A human presses a button to mark forming start and end, and the stated purpose is a digital twin to optimise heating and spinning actuation for quality and throughput ([Zenodo 17226615](https://zenodo.org/records/17226615), [V]). The IoPT repository paper lists predictive monitoring, but not prescriptive monitoring, among the intended uses ([CEUR](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3469/paper-16.pdf), [S]). One inference matters for the experimental design: in the cotton candy log, the forming-start and forming-end events *are* the human operator's start/stop actions. The event-aligned decision grid is therefore partly generated by the behaviour policy itself. That is informative sampling in the TESAR-CDE sense ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/vanderschueren23a.html), [V]), and it means the event grid and the confounding are entangled.

## Finer decisions help expressiveness but hurt estimation, with an optimum in between

The RL and causal-inference literatures together give the granularity question a precise shape. The starting point is a formal one. Event-aligned decisions are a semi-MDP over the sensor-level MDP, with options of the form "wait until the next event" (Sutton, Precup & Singh 1999; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(99)00052-1), [S]). **The sensor-level policy class therefore contains the event-level class**, so any loss from finer granularity is an estimation or optimisation effect, not an expressiveness effect. That framing turns "does sensor-time beat event-time?" into "when does the extra expressiveness outweigh the extra estimation cost?"

The estimation costs are well documented. As the step dt shrinks, advantages scale as O(dt), Q collapses onto V, and per-step Q-learning loses its signal (Baird 1994, [S]; Tallec, Blier & Ollivier 2019, [arXiv 1901.09732](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09732), [S]). Policy-gradient variance diverges (Munos 2006, [JMLR](https://jmlr.org/papers/v7/munos06b.html), [S]; Park et al. 2021, [arXiv 2111.03941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03941), [S]). Dithering exploration fails (Dabney et al. 2021, [arXiv 2006.01782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01782), [S]). With γ = e^(−dt/τ) the horizon in steps grows as τ/dt, and bootstrapped values propagate correspondingly slowly. Remedies include continuous-time formulations (Doya 2000; Jia & Zhou's q-learning, [arXiv 2207.00713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00713), [S]), dt-scaled N-step returns (Burns et al., [V]), and learned durations: FiGAR ([1702.06054](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.06054), [S]), TempoRL ([2106.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05262), [S]), SEAC/MOSEAC ([2401.09286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09286), [2406.01521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01521), [V]), TARC ([2510.23176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23176), [V]) and Lazy-MDPs ([2203.08542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08542), [V]). These consistently reduce decision counts at equal or better return, though almost always online. The direct consequence for the thesis is that **a naive per-tick learner with unchanged γ is expected to lose to event-aligned decisions for known, fixable reasons**. A fair comparison must rescale the discount and hyperparameters (Farrahi & Mahmood 2023, [arXiv 2305.05760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05760), [S]). Otherwise it measures a known artefact, not granularity.

Off-policy evaluation adds a second, sharper cost. Trajectory importance sampling has variance that can grow exponentially in the number of decision points (Liu et al. 2018, [1810.12429](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12429), [S]). Guo, Thomas & Brunskill show that evaluating option-level rather than primitive policies can cut that variance exponentially ([arXiv 1703.03453](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03453), [V]). Coarse grids, in turn, add bias, as Frost et al. demonstrate. For a stopping policy, the IS weight is a product over every "continue" tick before the stop, so halving dt roughly doubles the number of factors. **A selector that ranks granularities by IS-based OPE will therefore be biased toward coarse grids through lower variance rather than higher value.** That is the strongest technical reason the selector needs DR/FQE, marginalised IS (Xie et al. 2019, [1906.03393](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03393), [S]; Kallus & Uehara, [1908.08526](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08526), [S]) or timing-aware estimators such as EDQ. COBS and DOPE add the empirical warning that no OPE estimator dominates ([1911.06854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06854), [S]; [2103.16596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16596), [S]).

Biostatistics reaches the same trade-off from the causal side. Finer grids reduce discretization and identification bias (Sun & Crawford; Schulam & Saria) but multiply IPW weights and practical positivity violations. Adams, Saria & Rosenblum compare IPW, g-computation and TMLE over up to 257 time points ([arXiv 2011.15099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.15099), [V]). Sofrygin et al. coarsen daily EHR data to 5- to 90-day intervals ([arXiv 1705.09874](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09874), [V]). Ferreira Guerra et al. propose cross-validated variance to pick the discretization ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8710), [V]), which is a direct precedent for a data-driven granularity selector, though for effect estimation of fixed regimes. Tools that soften the fine-grid side include stabilised continuous-time weights (SCIP-Net, [2410.03514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03514), [V]; Ryalen et al., [1802.01946](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01946), [V]), incremental and modified treatment policies (Kennedy 2019, [1704.00211](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00211), [S]; Díaz et al. 2021, [2006.01366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01366), [S]), and neural CDE outcome models that evaluate potential outcomes at any time (TE-CDE, [2206.08311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08311), [V]). Shift-type estimands are especially relevant because operators keep machines inside narrow ranges, which makes deterministic "set to x at time t" regimes poorly supported.

The two literatures support one testable hypothesis. Estimated policy value should show an **interior optimum in decision granularity, and the optimum should move finer as the dataset grows**. That is what Zhang et al. prove for MC evaluation, and it is consistent with Metelli's persistence results. The thesis's selector can be judged by whether it recovers the oracle-optimal grid in the semi-synthetic benchmark.

## Neighbouring fields already act per sample, but with models, simulators or one-shot decisions

Process-control RL acts at every sampling interval as a matter of course, so the thesis cannot claim that deciding at sensor granularity is new in general. The reviews by Yoo et al. ([KAIST](https://koasas.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/291729), [V]), Nian et al. and Shin et al. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compchemeng.2019.05.029), [S]) describe RL with NMPC as the baseline. Yoo et al.'s phase-segmented Monte-Carlo DDPG for batch reactors ([PDF](https://dspace.kaist.ac.kr/bitstream/10203/280027/1/1-s2.0-S0098135420307912-main.pdf), [V]) is the nearest control analogue to event-aligned decision points. Offline RL from historical batch data runs into out-of-distribution action failures (Wang et al., DYCOPS 2025, [PDF](https://skoge.folk.ntnu.no/prost/proceedings/dycops-2025/DYCOPS2025_offline_program/contents/papers/0110.pdf), [S]). NeoRL-2 finds that current offline methods often fail to beat the conservative behaviour policy that collected the data ([arXiv 2503.19267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19267), [V]). This community takes the time grid as given and generally assumes a model or simulator. It does not learn from confounded logs or study grid choice. Kim et al.'s injection-moulding DRL, which combines quality, mould wear and time-of-use electricity cost ([arXiv 2505.10988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10988), [S]), is the closest objective to the cotton candy case's weight-versus-energy trade-off.

Manufacturing causal ML does policy learning, but for one fixed decision. Schwarz et al. use DML to derive lot-level rework policies in semiconductor manufacturing, reporting a 2–3% yield gain ([arXiv 2406.11308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11308), [V]). The INFORMS IJDS paper on doubly robust process-parameter policy learning ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/ijds.2024.0041), [S]) and the KU Leuven preventive-maintenance frequency optimisation of Vanderschueren et al. ([arXiv 2206.01562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01562v1), [V]) are both static. Otherwise, causality in manufacturing is mostly discovery (Vuković & Thalmann 2022, [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/c82fc8b4147549c8b388b52c7657cc26), [V]). Industrial "when to stop" is handled as detect, predict, then threshold. Mählkvist et al. cut batch-rejection cost to 26% of accept-all with classifiers plus cost thresholds, and found that **sensor-trajectory features did not improve accuracy** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10708579/), [V]). That negative result is useful: the value of sensor resolution cannot be assumed. Condition-based maintenance RL frames timing as optimal stopping on a degradation signal, but uses simulated models.

Optimal stopping and early classification of time series supply the methods for the stop decision. Longstaff–Schwartz ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/14.1.113), [S]) and Deep Optimal Stopping ([1804.05394](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05394), [S]) learn continuation values on a fixed exercise grid. Renault et al.'s ECTS survey frames early classification as a special case of optimal stopping ([2406.18332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18332), [V]). Kuchko's DO-IQS recovers stopping regions offline from expert stop times without a reward ([2503.03515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03515), [V]). All of these presuppose a fixed decision grid, and none studies how that grid's resolution affects learnability from observational data. Combined with Nie et al.'s starting rules and arXiv 2404.00221's constrained DTR treatment of starting and stopping ([V]), start-plus-stop is a two-stopping-time switching problem that no work addresses jointly with sensor covariates. Control theory adds the classic precedent that event-triggered (Lebesgue) sampling can beat periodic sampling at the same average rate (Åström & Bernhardsson 2002, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/CDC.2002.1184824), [S]). Process events behave like an exogenous triggering rule and sensor ticks like periodic sampling, and the theory says the better choice depends on the dynamics, not on which grid is finer.

The original dose-response topic remains a live secondary thread. Statistical estimators run from GPS (Hirano & Imbens) to doubly robust kernel curves (Kennedy et al. 2017, [1507.00747](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.00747), [S]). Neural ones include DRNet, VCNet ([2103.07861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07861), [V]) and KU Leuven's CBRNet (TMLR 2025, [2309.03731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03731), [V]). Continuous-action policy learning has kernel DR estimators (Kallus & Zhou 2018, [S]) and overlap-restricted dosage optimisation (Schweisthal et al. 2023, [V]). All of it is static, one decision per unit, and benchmarked on medical or text semi-synthetic data. The joint dose-and-time literature is thin: Soleimani et al. 2017's GP treatment-response with continuous doses at continuous times ([1704.02038](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02038), [S]), "Beyond the ATE … over Dose and Time" ([2507.07271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07271), [V]), and LMTP. A setpoint that can change at any sensor reading is a time-varying continuous treatment, the meeting point of both strands, and nobody has studied it on process data.

## Eight gaps the thesis can occupy

The evidence supports eight gaps, in order of how central they are to the RQ. **First**, no PresPM method defines decision points by time, sensor reading or learned triggers; SCOPE, Bozorgi and Weytjens are all event-aligned. **Second**, no prescriptive method of any kind runs on IoT-enriched event logs, even though formats (DataStream, CORE) and datasets (IoPT, cotton candy) exist. **Third**, no study varies decision granularity for *offline policy learning on logged, confounded data*. Zhang et al. is theory for evaluation, Metelli et al. vary uniform persistence, Frost et al. study clinical binning, and the biostatistics studies estimate fixed regimes. **Fourth**, no OPE-based selector compares non-uniform event grids with uniform sensor grids, and no work analyses the selection bias of IS-type estimators toward coarse grids. **Fifth**, no benchmark contains sensor streams with ground-truth effects of intervention *timing*. SimBank, SimBPIC17 and ProCause lack sensors, and causalAssembly and CausalMan benchmark discovery. **Sixth**, joint start-and-stop (switching) policies learned offline with high-frequency covariates are rare, since Nie et al. cover starting and the stopping literature assumes fixed grids. **Seventh**, dt-robust offline RL with discrete actions from irregular multivariate streams is missing; the dt-robustness results are online and mostly continuous-action. **Eighth**, continuous treatments are absent from PresPM, and time-varying continuous treatments are absent from industrial applications.

The novelty claim also faces three real threats. Process-control RL already decides per sample. Metelli et al. already select a temporal granularity from batch data. And the searches were not exhaustive sweeps of BPM, ICPM and CAiSE 2025–2026 proceedings, ML4PM, or NeurIPS and ICML 2026, with the Bertrand/Serral/De Smedt IoT group being the most likely source of an unseen IoT-*prescriptive* paper. The thesis should claim the combination, not any single ingredient.

## Conclusion

Deciding at sensor time is not a question of whether finer is better. Formally the finer policy class can only help, so whatever advantage event-aligned decisions keep comes from estimation: horizon length, overlap per tick, OPE variance, and the fact that in logs like the cotton candy one the events are themselves the operator's actions. The thesis can therefore turn a vague "does IoT help PresPM?" into a sharp, falsifiable claim: under a fixed log budget, policy value is non-monotone in decision granularity, the optimum moves finer with more data, and a selector built on horizon-robust OPE recovers it, whereas an IS-based selector systematically picks too coarse. Confirming or refuting that claim on a ground-truth benchmark would be a contribution even if the selector itself turned out to be simple.

The design choices follow from the literature. Use dt-rescaled discounts and N-step returns so that the comparison does not measure a known artefact. Include the behaviour policy, "stop at end", SCOPE on the event grid, Nie-style DR when-to-treat learning, FQI, IQL and CQL as baselines. Sweep behaviour-policy stochasticity in the style of CRN's gamma, so that coverage limits are separated from learner quality. Evaluate the OPE estimators themselves against the oracle. These are what would let the result stand up to the SCOPE, Metelli and Frost comparisons an examiner will make.
